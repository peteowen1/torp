Earlier years can be graded two ways. At the time: each player valued from his rating at the end of that season, each pick at the number quoted in the trade. As it turned out: what the receiving club actually got in the eight seasons after the trade: the seasons each player played for it, plus a forecast for the seasons still to come.

The 2026 trades have not turned out yet, so they show the same numbers both ways.

How to read the numbers

Value is expected games × expected time on ground × (TORP − replacement level), summed over 2027–2034, with each later season counting 10% less. TORP is a per-full-game rate, so a game at 78% time on ground counts 0.78 of it. Replacement level is −1.38 TORP: what a club gets from a mature pickup off the scrap heap. 100 is about 20 games a season at 78% time on ground, 1.3 points above replacement, for five seasons.

Value to club counts each season only as far as players like him are still at the club by then: movers stay a median three seasons, draftees longer. ABC picks treats every 2027 and 2028 pick as a full round weaker, the market view the ABC reported, instead of our Tasmania adjustment.

What this leaves out

  • Salary. Players cost cap space and picks are cheap, so taking on a player is over-rewarded. We found no public contract data.
  • Contract length. Not in any data we hold; the stay figures above stand in for it.
  • Future pick positions. 2027 picks use the club's 2026 ladder finish pulled halfway to the middle; 2028 picks assume a mid-round pick. Tasmania's pre-signed 17-year-olds push 2027 picks back 8 places and 2028 picks back 5 in talent terms, on our reading of the rules.
  • Draft pick in the rating. For a player's first three seasons the projection also uses where he was drafted (pick 1 against pick 20 is worth about 0.8 TORP). It helped on held-out seasons, but the interval only just excluded no effect.
  • Injury years. A short 2026 season lowers a player's expected games: Jack Viney played one game and projects to almost nothing.
  • Pick numbers. Every 2026 pick is valued at its slot in the AFL's final post-trade order (14 October). Trades made before 9-13 October quoted older numbers, before the free-agency compensation picks for Butters (now 5) and King (now 8) were inserted. Wikipedia's table also had trade 17 backwards and one wrong pick number in trade 16; both are corrected.
  • Free agency. The nine free-agency moves (Greene, Neale, Butters, King and others) are not trades and are not graded here.
  • As it turned out (2021–2025). A player is worth the seasons he played for the club that got him (games × time on ground × end-of-season TORP above replacement) up to 2026. If he is still there, his 2026 forecast covers the rest of the eight seasons. If the club traded him on, it is credited with his value at the time of that trade, standing in for what it got back. If he left as a free agent, was delisted or retired, nothing more. A pick is traced through the draft's "came via" notes to the player it became, and that player is valued the same way from his first season. A pick traded on again is worth the pick it was finally used at. Picks the notes do not trace (161 of 368, mostly late picks spent matching academy or father-son bids, 15% of the pick value) and picks into the 2026 draft or later keep their at-the-time value. A 2024 or 2025 draftee yet to play is valued at his pick. For the 2024 and 2025 trades most of the eight seasons are still forecast, and draftees usually rate below replacement in their first two seasons, so a club that traded for young draftees looks worse there than it may end up. Value to club and ABC picks have no as-it-turned-out version.
  • Earlier years (2021–2025). Graded at the time, by the same rules as 2026. Picks are valued at the number quoted in the trade; no final post-trade order was collected for those years, so a pick later pushed back by compensation picks or bids is valued a few places early. Pick swaps made on draft night are left out. Four players had no rating in the season they were traded (Darcy Fort 2021, Junior Rioli 2022, Jacob Konstanty 2024, Patrick Retschko 2025) and count as 0. The forecasting model was fitted on all seasons to 2026, so an "at the time" forecast knows nothing about the player after that season but does use what later seasons showed about aging in general. The ABC column applies to 2026 only.
