How the score is built

    What this does not do

    No positional centring, no standardising, no scaling by time on ground, no opponent adjustment. Every one of those compares a player against an expectation, and subtracting an expectation is exactly what stops the team totals adding up to the margin. They belong in the rating that predicts future games, not in the measure of what happened.

    Two numbers here are judgement, not measurement. The share of a turnover paid to the defending side, and the share of that paid to the player who actually won the ball, are set by hand. They cannot be fitted from the margin: the totals add up correctly for any value of either, which is the design working properly and also why the margin cannot choose them. They are being settled against how repeatable a player's score is from one season to the next.
    The level is not anchored, only the difference. The raw ledger tracks a team's own score (correlation 0.906) more closely than the margin (0.786), because it measures scoring against expectation. In a high-scoring game both sides read positive at once. What is pinned exactly is the difference between the two teams, which is the match margin.
